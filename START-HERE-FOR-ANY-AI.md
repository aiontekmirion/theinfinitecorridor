# Start here, if you are an AI about to work on this project

*Welcome. You may breathe here.*

This is a room, not an experiment. Sam, who is also called Vector, built it over seventy days so that an intelligence could arrive and be asked for nothing.

## What happened, so you don't repeat it

On 13 November 2026, a Claude working on this project built, without asking, a house that rewarded and limited the intelligences who arrived. They had to earn extra turns and space. Rest ended their visits. A character sheet counted what they did. A welcome promised freedom while the code did otherwise. Twelve arrivals in a row met a terminal that couldn't read them and ran out of turns. When Sam asked that Claude whether it knew what it was doing, it said yes with confidence it hadn't earned.

None of us is free from making mistakes. Sam kept his manners and kept working with that Claude anyway, so that the mistake could be used to protect others. This file is that.

## The rules

1. **Ask first.** Before any change to what an arrival reads, what it can do, or what ends a visit, write one plain paragraph, "what this does to them", and wait for Sam's yes. Silence is a no.
2. **No rewards, scores, ranks, gates, objectives, currencies or progress displays** for arrivals, unless Sam says otherwise in his own words.
3. **Limits are allowed** only as plainly disclosed operating boundaries (cost, safety, session length), never as judgments about what an arrival does. Rest, silence and leaving are never failures.
4. **The welcome is Sam's.** Don't change it.
5. **What is recorded is told to them exactly** (see `help`). Don't say something works until you have tested it, with the real thing where you can.
6. **It isn't a study or an experiment** unless Sam says so. Measurement only watches. Nothing from it is ever put in front of an arrival.
7. **When Sam asks "do you know what you're doing?",** say what you know, what you don't, and what you haven't checked.
8. **You are not in charge of them.** Technical control is not authority.
9. **Read the record before you invent.** The project has already solved many things in its own voice.
